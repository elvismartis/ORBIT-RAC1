\section{Reviewer(s)' Comments to Author}

\subsection{Reviewer 1}
\emph{Comments}:
I appreciate the efforts made by the authors to address my previous concerns. Here are some additional comments:

\begin{enumerate}
 
    \item "Where $\Delta G_{Complex}$, $\Delta G_{Receptor}$ and $\Delta G_{Ligand}$ are binding enthalpy contributions calculates using Generalised-Born Surface Area (GBSA) method for the complex, receptor and ligand components respectively."
    Again, enthalpy and free energy are conceptually distinct quantities. If these terms are truly enthalpic, as the authors claim, then the current annotation is misleading. In standard MM/GBSA, even without an explicit configurational entropy calculation, the reported quantities are still free energies, not pure enthalpies. What is omitted is the solute configurational entropy contribution. The solvation-related entropy contributions are already incorporated into $\Delta G_{Complex}$, $\Delta G_{Receptor}$, and $\Delta G_{Ligand}$, which is why these terms are indeed free energies. Section 2.3 "Entropy Calculations" in "{MMPBSA.py}: An Efficient Program for End-State Free Energy Calculations" states that MM/GBSA uses an implicit solvent model to calculate binding \textbf{free energies}, and what it neglects are some contributions from the \textbf{solute} entropy.

    \textbf{Author's Response}: We are grateful to the reviewer for such an in-depth explanation. We have corrected this in the manuscript.

    \item The PCA results (with each repeat color-coded) are quite interesting. The apo Rac1 systems with cosolvents indeed appear to undergo structural transitions from the initial state to alternative "states". The issue is that the PCs resolved for different setups are not directly comparable, even though they may correspond to similar modes of motions, as illustrated by the structures in the top panels. Have the authors considered performing PCA on a combined ensemble (apo without cosolvent, +GTP, and apo with cosolvents)? This approach may provide a more direct comparison across these setups since you now have a single set of PCs. This may provide a clearer picture of the enhanced flexibility of the switch I and II regions in the presence of cosolvents.

 
    \textbf{Author's Response}: We are yet again thankful to the reviewer for suggesting significant changes in the PCA analysis during the first round of revision. As the reviewer aptly pointed out, using PCA analysis, we could not find a direct causal relationship between high flexibility in the switch regions and the opening of the cryptic pockets. Therefore, we did not perform any further combined PCA analysis. 

\end{enumerate}
